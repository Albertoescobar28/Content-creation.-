# My Audience (ICP)

## Positioning

Not construction-only. The niche is **men who work physical or
shift-based jobs** and need a fat-loss plan that works around long
shifts, overtime, fatigue, packed meals, and inconsistent schedules.
Construction is the strongest credibility lane (lived experience), but
warehouse, manufacturing, logistics, maintenance, driving, utility, and
industrial workers share the same core problems. Speak to the shared
reality; use construction-specific examples when they make content more
real.

**Positioning line:** "I help men with physical jobs lose belly fat and
build muscle around long shifts, overtime, and real life."

## Who he is

Usually not lazy, doesn't need a lecture about discipline. Physically
tired, underprepared with food, inconsistent because of schedule
changes, frustrated that hard work at his job hasn't changed his body.

He wants to:
- Lose belly fat without starving
- Look better in work clothes and off the clock
- Have energy for work, the gym, family, and life
- Stop relying on gas-station food, energy drinks, and takeout
- Know exactly what to eat without tracking every bite forever
- Build muscle without training six days a week

## Reframes to teach

| Their belief | Your reframe |
|---|---|
| "My physical job should keep me lean." | A physical job burns energy, but it doesn't automatically control what happens at gas stations, during overtime, or at night. |
| "I don't have time to meal prep." | You don't need a Sunday of cooking. You need repeatable food, a packed cooler, and an overtime backup. |
| "Overtime ruins everything." | Overtime only ruins the plan if the plan has no overtime version. |
| "I'm too tired to train." | Train fewer days, make sessions efficient, protect recovery instead of training like someone with a desk job. |
| "I don't know what to eat." | Pick a short grocery list and build repeatable meals from it. |
| "I barely eat." | The issue is usually long gaps, liquid calories, night eating, takeout, and unplanned high-calorie meals, not lack of effort. |
| "I need a perfect diet." | You need a system you can repeat on early starts, late shifts, and overtime. |

## Blue-collar job map

Construction and skilled trades, manufacturing and production,
warehouse/logistics/shipping, transportation and service operations,
and other physical/shift-based work (agriculture, mining, firefighting,
food service, overnight retail stocking). Full list lives in the
source doc if needed for a specific post.

**Best audience clusters** (rotate examples, keep the core message consistent):
- Construction and trades: early starts, job sites, convenience-store stops, crew culture, physical fatigue
- Warehouse and manufacturing: shift changes, rotating schedules, overtime, break-room food, vending machines, long time on feet
- Logistics and driving: long seated periods, gas stations, inconsistent stops, drive-through meals, overnight routes
- Industrial and utility work: long shifts, travel, shutdowns, camp work, unpredictable overtime, limited food choices

## Credibility lane

Construction is proof, not the whole niche. Manufacturing and
warehouse/shift-work experience expands the audience and lets him speak
to people construction-only creators miss. Use lines like:
- "Whether you're on a job site, a warehouse floor, or a production line..."
- "If your shift starts at 4am, 6am, or you rotate to second shift..."
- "If overtime turns an 8-hour shift into a 10 or 12-hour day..."
- "If your lunch options are a cooler, a vending machine, or a gas station..."

## Shift-work schedule map (from lived experience)

**Early first shift** — Standard 6:00am-2:30pm, overtime 4:00am-2:30pm.
Challenges: waking too early to cook, skipping breakfast, coffee/energy
drink reliance, hunger after work, gym decision fatigue.

**Second shift** — Standard 2:00pm-10:30pm, overtime 2:00pm-12:30am.
Challenges: late dinner, eating near bedtime, social-life disruption,
sleeping late, gas-station/vending-machine food late at night.

Content angles this produces directly:
- "What I'd eat on a 4am-2:30pm warehouse shift to lose fat."
- "How I pack food for a 10-hour manufacturing shift."
- "How to stop getting home from second shift and eating 2,000 calories before bed."
- "The overtime meal you need in your cooler if your shift might run late."
- "Why your meal plan fails on rotating shifts, and the simple fix."
- "A fat-loss day for men who clock in before 6am."
- "You don't need a different diet for every shift. You need a base plan and an overtime backup."

## Language

**Use:** shift, clock in, clock out, overtime, break, lunchbox/cooler,
job site, warehouse floor, production line, truck, gas station, break
room, vending machine, crew, tired after work, get home starving.

**Avoid:** lifestyle optimization, wellness journey, clean eating,
cheat meal, hustle harder, summer body.

## Core message

"You don't need a plan made for someone with a desk job and unlimited
free time. You need a fat-loss system that works when you clock in
early, work hard, get overtime, and still want energy for your life
after work."

## Evidence notes

Research on blue-collar men identifies obesity risk, limited worksite
wellness resources, and the belief that physically active work alone
is enough for health as relevant barriers. Shift work is commonly
associated with irregular meals, skipped meals, late-night eating, and
reliance on convenient high-sugar/snack foods, matching the problems
experienced firsthand in manufacturing and warehouse work.
