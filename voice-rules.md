# Universal Voice Rules

## How to read this file
This is the agency's universal voice contract, distilled from everything Briar has taught across 120 lectures about how content should read, sound, and be written. These rules apply to any content generated for any student. A student's personal voice rules (in `my-brand/voice.md`) layer on top of these and take precedence where they conflict.

## Hard rules (never broken)

### No em-dashes
This is an absolute formatting rule for all generated copy.

### On-screen text hooks stay at nine words max
Beyond nine words there is a steep falloff in performance (Briar attributes this to shared psychology with email subject lines). His most viral videos run about eight words on the main on-screen hook. **Source**: 2025-09-17, 2025-11-19, 2025-11-20

### Speak at a fifth grade reading level
Overwhelm is one of the main reasons a viewer quits. Losing someone on even two words causes a bounce. Simple language wins even for sophisticated, bottom-of-funnel buyers. It takes more intelligence to speak simply. **Source**: 2025-10-24, 2025-11-21, 2025-11-22

### On-screen text must be an open loop, never a restatement
The on-screen text hook should allude to something delivered later in the video. It should never repeat what you say in the verbal hook. Repeating the on-screen text verbatim in the spoken hook is one of the biggest mistakes creators make, because the mind can process reading and listening simultaneously. **Source**: 2025-09-08, 2025-09-17, 2026-06-12

### Never resolve the hook or curiosity loop too early
If the on-screen text alludes to something later, nothing in the first 15 to 20 seconds should accidentally satisfy that curiosity. Once you pop a curiosity loop, you must immediately reopen a new one in the next sentence or the viewer is gone, even loyal fans. **Source**: 2025-09-17, 2025-09-24, 2025-10-23, 2025-10-29, 2026-05-08

### Every claim should be falsifiable, visual, and unique
Run every sentence against three questions: (1) Is it falsifiable (objectively provable true or false)? (2) Can I point at it or see it? (3) Has no one else said this before? You should fight to get every sentence as close to these as possible. **Source**: 2025-11-21, 2025-11-22

## Strong defaults

### Two lines of on-screen text is the sweet spot
Briar's gut and testing lean toward two-line on-screen hooks over three lines (a 5x difference in one Jefferson Fisher / Chris Williamson test). Never use three lines unless broken up with indentation or parentheses. Hold this loosely; it is a strong default, not an absolute. **Source**: 2025-09-08, 2025-10-31, 2026-05-06, 2026-05-08

### Write hooks like a text message, not a memo
The more casual and text-message-like the tone, the better a hook performs. Formal tone hurts. Use lowercase, abbreviations like "ppl," phrases like "lol." Briar is skeptical of emojis unless a perfect fit. **Source**: 2025-11-19, 2025-11-20

### Lead with nouns, not verbs or function words
Load hooks with proper nouns, numbers, and vivid nouns. Get the most important word as close to word one as possible; filler at the front costs you the early retention cliff. Keep function words (is, the, has, your, not, if, but, and, so, by) under roughly 30 to 40 percent of on-screen text words. Identity-driven framing ("practice owner") beats verb-driven framing. **Source**: 2025-10-24, 2025-10-31, 2025-11-21, 2025-11-22, 2026-02-04, 2026-02-05

### "How" beats "why" in on-screen hooks
Action-oriented framing ("how to X," "what," "when") outperforms philosophical framing ("why I do X"). People want tactics, not philosophy. **Source**: 2025-10-27, 2025-10-31

### Make it tangible, not abstract
Tie abstract concepts to concrete units: specific numbers, timeframes, named frameworks, or observable situations. Replace vague words with something the viewer can grab or feel. **Source**: 2025-09-08, 2025-09-15, 2025-10-20, 2025-10-23, 2025-10-24

### Take a definitive stance; avoid hedging
People follow opinions, stances, and statements. Draw straight lines. Nuance kills retention and dilutes follower quality. Put edge cases and disclaimers in the caption, not the script. Critique principles, not people. **Source**: 2025-09-24, 2025-10-24, 2025-10-27, 2025-10-29, 2025-11-19, 2026-05-11

### Lean authentic over over-scripted
When forced to choose between a perfectly engineered hook and what you would naturally say, lean authentic. Bake in at most one psychological principle per hook, keep it under three to five seconds, but do not sacrifice the natural voice. As AI and content saturation rise, audiences increasingly auto-bounce on over-engineered hooks. **Source**: 2025-11-17, 2025-11-18, 2026-07-06

### On-screen text should read like a title, not a statement
It works better when it reads like a book chapter title (dynamic, curiosity-carrying) than a flat declarative sentence. Avoid pure declaratives, which close the loop instead of opening it. Reframe as questions, contrasts, or relationships ("the difference between X and Y"). **Source**: 2025-12-17, 2026-05-08

### Don't say more than you have, but say as much as you have
Video length is a function of pacing skill, not a target. Detach from length. Be brutally honest about whether a point is genuinely strong or just filler. Cut roughly 20 percent from the first draft. **Source**: 2025-09-24, 2025-10-27, 2025-11-17, 2025-11-18

## Vocabulary to avoid

- **"Success"** — too unfalsifiable and subjective; take it out of your vocabulary entirely. **Source**: 2025-11-21, 2025-11-22
- **"Great," "better," "more," "improve," "things"** — too vague; attach a concrete unit or outcome instead. Never use "better" without saying what specifically improves. **Source**: 2025-10-20, 2025-10-23
- **"Powerful"** — too subjective, same bucket as "success." **Source**: 2025-11-22
- **Hyper-definitive clickbait words** ("biggest," "most important," "number one," "top 1%," "worst") — use with caution; they can read as clickbait. **Source**: 2025-10-29
- **Manipulative curiosity crutches** ("stop scrolling," "the last tip is the most important," "stay until the end") — overplayed; they increase bounce and cost brand credibility. **Source**: 2025-09-10, 2025-10-24
- **Comparative offer language** ("smarter," "better," "cheaper," "faster," "the best") — implies competition when naming or claiming an offer's niche. **Source**: 2026-07-03
- **Canned hook templates / hook swipe files** — the internet moves too fast, and hooks that "try too hard" trigger the opposite reaction. Think in hook concepts (pain point, contrarian take, dream scenario), not recycled lines. **Source**: 2025-10-17, 2025-10-20

## Sentence structure preferences

- **Get into your first sentence within three seconds.** Don't start talking five to seven seconds in. Finish your opening sentence fast, then explain in sentence two or three. **Source**: 2025-10-27, 2026-02-04, 2026-02-05
- **Storytelling is the speed of information.** Spend only one to two sentences per idea, then move to the next pocket. Keep the viewer's mental picture refreshing. Progress through 3 to 6 distinct scenes or information pockets per video. **Source**: 2025-09-24, 2025-11-21, 2025-11-22, 2025-11-27
- **Never more than about three sentences in one caption paragraph.** Use enters, indents, bullets, quotes, parentheses, bolds, italics, all caps. Write for skimming, not linear reading. **Source**: 2025-09-17
- **Reinforce with examples, don't restate with synonyms.** Explaining something more clearly with an example is good; saying the same idea three or four different ways is not. **Source**: 2025-10-23
- **"But" is the most powerful word in content.** It opens and closes curiosity loops. Also lean on "but this is where it gets crazy" and "you might think it's X, but it's actually Y" as pattern-interrupt beats. **Source**: 2025-10-24, 2025-12-15, 2025-12-17
- **Address and dress up the elephant in the room.** Point out the obvious that no one is talking about, then make it look beautiful. **Source**: 2026-07-06
- **Fight for uniqueness, bonus points if only you can say it.** A claim only you can personally make ("what I learned working with X") is the strongest version. Don't chase uniqueness at the cost of accuracy or value. **Source**: 2025-11-21, 2026-04-03
- **Use "communal language" for shares.** Write to a group ("you and your partner," "you and your videographer") rather than a single person when the goal is sends. **Source**: 2025-10-15

## Format-specific rules

### On-screen text (hook)
Nine words max, ideally around eight, two lines. Text-message tone. Open loop that never restates the verbal hook. Noun-heavy, low function words. Read it like a book title. May optionally carry a short parenthetical "whisper" hitting one of curiosity, risk reversal, or social proof. Keep no more than three focal elements on screen at once (borrowed from YouTube thumbnail psychology). Hold on-screen text roughly 7 to 10 seconds, not the app default of 3; no one scrolls because text stayed up too long. **Source**: 2025-09-08, 2025-09-17, 2025-10-20, 2025-11-19, 2025-11-20, 2026-02-04, 2026-02-05, 2026-05-08

### Verbal hook vs on-screen text (coverage rule)
The two are a paradox pair. Whichever leans curiosity, the other should lean clarity. Do not overlap what they communicate; each should cover a different chunk of the video's context so together they preview as much of the video as possible in the first three to five seconds. Write the verbal hook right before recording; write the on-screen text last (after recording), so you can plant curiosity that complements what you actually said. **Source**: 2025-09-17, 2025-10-20, 2025-11-22, 2026-05-08

### Captions
Always include captions (roughly 30 percent of viewers watch with no audio). Use the platform-native default style (white bar top, white bordered font). Treat the caption as a complementary second hook: put credibility and social proof there, or comment bait, context, or elaboration. Compress so the most important words land in the first six. If revenue is the goal, put the CTA in the first sentence of the caption (over 80 percent see the caption, only ~5 percent finish the video). Avoid the red bouncing TikTok bubble and one-word/one-letter animated captions; they signal beginner and underperform. **Source**: 2025-10-06, 2025-10-20, 2025-10-24, 2025-10-29, 2025-12-15, 2026-05-04, 2026-05-08

### Carousels
Slide 1 must be a hook. Slide 2 must be a super hook that stands alone (Evidence Without Context), because the algorithm gives carousels a second chance starting at slide 2. Carousels must have music or they won't reach the discovery feed. Each slide should be independently shareable to a relatable audience segment (shareability is the underlying principle behind why AI carousels work). Carousels are bottom-of-funnel weapons that reach the most existing followers. **Source**: 2025-10-29, 2025-12-15, 2025-12-16, 2026-04-29

### Reaction / commentary videos
Start the first sentence within three seconds. Keep the clip you're reacting to short (about 10 to 15 seconds). Pacing must be fast and punchy (observe, reply, observe, reply). Always add something new or you won't gain followers. "But" is the primary rehook word. **Source**: 2025-12-15, 2026-02-04, 2026-02-05

### Bios (three-sentence formula)
Sentence 1: how you help them ("I make content for X who want Y"). Sentence 2: your unique value proposition (title, social proof, or experience). Sentence 3: a clear, simple next step (DM me, click link, follow). **Source**: 2025-10-27, 2026-02-20

### Scripts (foundational body blueprint)
Hook, super hook, explain (1 to 3 sentences of the thesis without giving the climax), illustrate (8 to 10 sentences via story, metaphor, statistic, or example), apply (one hyper-tactical, unique action step). Good as training wheels but should be no more than about a third of videos. Reopen a fresh curiosity loop and repeat the explain-illustrate-apply cycle rather than stretching one loop too long. End on the climax; cut the summary and falling action. **Source**: 2025-09-12, 2025-12-10, 2025-11-24, 2025-11-21

### Prompting an LLM (voice for AI-assisted drafting)
Do not ask for "write me a script." Give it your beliefs about the topic, your definition of good output, the end objective, and two to three examples. AI is strong on structure but weak on pacing and flow, so never take its scripting word for word. **Source**: 2025-11-10, 2026-02-20, 2026-05-08
